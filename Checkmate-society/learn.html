Learn Chess

Know the pieces, then build a plan

Chess becomes easier to understand when you first learn what each piece can do and then use a simple plan for each stage of the game.

Interactive Piece Guide

Meet the pieces

Select any piece to open its movement rule.

♚King

Moves one square in any direction. It may not move onto a square attacked by an opponent.

♛Queen

Moves any number of open squares along a rank, file, or diagonal.

♜Rook

Moves any number of open squares horizontally or vertically.

♝Bishop

Moves any number of open squares diagonally and stays on the same color of square.

♞Knight

Moves to a square two files and one rank away, or two ranks and one file away. It can jump over pieces.

♟Pawn

Moves forward one square, may move two from its starting square if clear, and captures one square diagonally forward.

Movement summaries are based on official chess rules. Review the US Chess learn-to-play guide.

A simple game plan

  1. Starting the game
    • Control the center with pawns and pieces.
    • Develop knights and bishops.
    • Castle early when it is safe.
  2. During the game
    • Check what your opponent is threatening.
    • Look for checks, captures, and attacks.
    • Improve pieces that are not active.
  3. Finishing the game
    • Activate the king in the endgame.
    • Push passed pawns carefully.
    • Look for checkmate before trading everything.

Remember

You do not need to memorize dozens of opening moves to enjoy chess. Start by developing your pieces, protecting your king, and paying attention to what your opponent can do next.
